Find alternative decoding in a genome

Codon Atlas aligns a six-frame translation of the genome to Pfam protein families and asks, for every one of the 64 codons, which amino acid the conserved columns it lands on expect. It also looks for stop codons inside conserved domains, reads the tRNA genes, and lists every affected site.

Stop codons inside conserved domains

Every TAA, TAG and TGA that sits on a confidently aligned consensus column of a Pfam domain. Many in-domain stops of one kind across many families mean a reassigned stop codon; a handful in specific families suggest selenocysteine, pyrrolysine, readthrough, or pseudogenes.

Positions

tRNA genes

Found with ARAGORN. Each anticodon is matched to the codons it can pair with (Watson–Crick and wobble). For tRNAs with arginine anticodons, identity elements are read as in Shulgina & Eddy (2021): A20 and the discriminator base N73 separate Arg-, Trp-, Met- and Gln-type acceptors. Positions are found by a structural heuristic, so treat calls as leads.

Sites & export

Pick a decoding hypothesis to list every in-frame site in the genes, with sequence context and, where the site falls in a Pfam domain, what that column expects. The database package names the hypothesis as codon-2-amino-acid (for example TGA2W) and is ready for a proteomics search, for example with Recode Detector.

Profile database

Codon Atlas needs Pfam once. The recommended set is rebuilt from Pfam's seed alignments without entropy weighting (as Codetta does), which keeps each column's amino-acid probabilities close to what the seed alignment shows.

How it works

Codon Atlas re-implements the inference model of Codetta (Shulgina & Eddy, eLife 2021; Codetta 2.0 in Shulgina's 2022 thesis) and adds stop-codon, tRNA and site-level views.

  1. Six-frame translation. The genome is cut into 100 kb pieces (3 kb overlap) and translated in all six frames with the standard code, stop codons written as X so nothing is assumed about them.
  2. Alignment. Pfam profile HMMs are searched against the translation with HMMER (pyhmmer). Domains with independent E-value below 10−10 are kept (E-values scaled to the number of profiles, as in hmmscan), and within them only consensus columns aligned with posterior probability ≥ 0.95.
  3. One column per codon. Each genomic codon keeps the column from its most significant domain. Families of mobile elements, viruses, selenoproteins, pyrrolysine proteins and mitochondrion-encoded proteins are left out of inference by default (identified by name and description).
  4. Decoding probabilities. For codon Z with aligned columns C1…CN, each amino-acid model a and the non-specific model ‘?’ score
    log P(C | a) − log P(C | ?) = Σi wi [ log eCi(a) − log ē(a) ]

    where e is the column's emission probability and ē(a) its average over every aligned column in this genome; w caps any single column at 1% of the codon's columns (each unique column once below 100). With a uniform prior over the 21 models the scores become probabilities; a codon is called when one amino acid exceeds 0.9999.
  5. Stability. The capped columns are resampled (bootstrap) and the share of resamples that keep the same best model is shown as support, which matters most for rare codons.
  6. Leads. When no model passes the threshold, the ratio between the best alternative amino acid and the standard one is still reported, so weak signals in rare codons are not lost.
  7. Stop codons. Every stop codon on an aligned consensus column is listed with what the column expects (all families, including the excluded ones, so selenoproteins and pyrrolysine proteins show up here).
  8. tRNAs. ARAGORN (pyaragorn) finds tRNA genes; anticodons are matched to codons with standard wobble rules (G34: C/U, U34: A/G, C34: G, A34 read as I34: U/C/A). For arginine-anticodon tRNAs, A20 and N73 give Arg/Trp/Met/Gln-type calls.
  9. Sites. In-frame occurrences in annotated (GenBank) or predicted (Prodigal) genes, with context and the Pfam column's probabilities for the standard and the alternative residue.

Known sources of error (from Codetta's authors): profile alignments to pseudogenes or non-coding sequence, extensive mRNA editing, and strongly unusual amino-acid composition. A predicted code is a hypothesis to test, ideally experimentally.

Software: HMMER 3 via pyhmmer (Larralde & Zeller 2023), Pfam (Mistry et al. 2021), ARAGORN via pyaragorn (Laslett & Canback 2004), Prodigal via pyrodigal (Hyatt et al. 2010; Larralde 2022).
